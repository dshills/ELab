# Beginner activities and everyday-use rehearsal

The owner has no electronics background or participants available. This milestone
uses engineering judgment and repeatable application walkthroughs instead of
requiring recruitment or expert feedback from the owner. No participant session
has been conducted, and no completion percentage or research finding is claimed.
The older ten-person first-use study remains historical proposed research.

Choose **Learn circuits & shortcuts** in the parts panel (or Help in the command
menu). Start an LED, capacitor or four-MOSFET H-bridge activity. The ready-made
circuit loads through normal guarded replacement/save/recovery controls. The
activity stays beside the drawing, explains one step at a time, and can be closed
without changing the circuit. Advancing a step records no learning score or
telemetry. Loading another example closes an unrelated guide.

The LED activity explains V, A, mA and kΩ, changes only the resistor and compares
current. The capacitor activity explains µF, pause/resume/restart, slower charging
and recording. The H-bridge activity explains the purpose of its four switches,
signed load readings and capturing both direction intervals. All finish with
project/recording ownership guidance.

Engineering rehearsal covers loading from Help, visible step navigation, an
actual resistor edit that reduces LED current, save/reload, and cancellation of
a replacement without losing the current activity or circuit. Existing browser
journeys cover exact captured playback, H-bridge fixtures, exports and recovery.
The app's models and numerical checks supply electrical evidence; they do not
prove a particular person's understanding.

## Reference decisions

Instructions identify each action, units and expected change rather than relying
on prior electronics knowledge. This applies W3C's [clear step-by-step
instructions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/patterns/o4p07-step-instructions/)
and [clear task steps](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/patterns/o1p04-clear-steps/).
The capacitor explanation uses the standard RC charging relationship: increasing
C at fixed R increases the time constant. See [OpenStax RC circuits](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/10-5-rc-circuits).
These are design and electrical references, not borrowed participant findings.
